\section{Introduction and Objectives}
In broadcast and shared-medium communications, multiple independent network nodes share a common physical channel to transmit data packets. Without centralized scheduling or coordinated arbitration, concurrent transmissions on the shared medium inevitably overlap in time and space, resulting in destructive interference known as a \textbf{packet collision}. When a collision occurs, colliding frames are rendered mutually unreadable, corrupted, and discarded by receivers, leading to substantial degradation of throughput, increased latency, and wasted bandwidth.

To resolve contention and allocate channel access fairly and efficiently among autonomous stations, the Data Link Layer---specifically the \textbf{Medium Access Control (MAC) sublayer}---employs Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA) techniques. CSMA mandates that a station sense the medium (carrier sensing) prior to transmitting, adopting the foundational principle ``listen before talk.''

The primary objectives of this laboratory assignment are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Emulation of a Shared Physical Channel Server:} Design and construct a multi-client socket-based shared channel emulator in C running over TCP/IP loopback (\texttt{127.0.0.1:5000}) that dynamically tracks and enforces the physical channel state machine among three distinct states: \textbf{IDLE}, \textbf{BUSY}, and \textbf{COLLISION}.
    \item \textbf{Implementation of Independent Station Processes:} Construct independent client processes emulating network stations implementing four canonical medium access strategies:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Non-Persistent CSMA:} Randomizes re-sensing intervals upon detecting a busy medium, reducing post-transmission collision bursts at the cost of idle channel slots.
        \item \textbf{1-Persistent CSMA:} Continuously monitors the medium and transmits with probability $p=1$ immediately when the medium becomes idle, maximizing channel utilization under low traffic but suffering severe collision cascades under heavy loads.
        \item \textbf{$p$-Persistent CSMA:} Balances the trade-off between idle channel waste and collision bursts by operating on slotted contention boundaries, transmitting with probability $p$ and deferring with probability $1-p$.
        \item \textbf{CSMA with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD):} Emulates IEEE 802.3 Ethernet behavior where transceivers monitor the medium while transmitting, immediately abort collided transmissions, emit a jamming signal, and schedule retransmissions using Truncated Binary Exponential Backoff (BEB).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{End-to-End Three-Tier Architecture:} Implement an end-to-end communication pipeline: Senders $\to$ Channel Server $\to$ Receiver, where clean frames are relayed to a dedicated destination node and acknowledgments are routed back for collision-detected transfers.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Performance Profiling:} Benchmark the protocols across the mandatory test cases:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Sweep persistence probability $p \in [0.05, 1.00]$ in $p$-Persistent CSMA for fixed station count ($N = 10$) to determine the optimal operating point $p^*$.
        \item Benchmark all four schemes across varying numbers of contending stations ($N \in [2, 30]$) to analyze collisions, average transmission delay, and channel throughput.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Theoretical Principles of Medium Access Control}

\subsection{Channel Propagation Delay and Normalized Parameter \texorpdfstring{$a$}{a}}
In any broadcast medium, electromagnetic signals propagate at a finite velocity $v$ (approximately $2 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$ in copper cabling). For two stations separated by distance $d$, the one-way propagation delay is:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = T_{\text{prop}} = \frac{d}{v}
\end{equation}
The vulnerability period of CSMA is $2\tau$: if Station A begins transmitting at time $t_0$, its signal does not reach Station B until $t_0 + \tau$. If Station B senses the channel at $t_0 + \tau - \epsilon$, it detects an IDLE medium and begins transmitting, causing a collision that corrupts both packets. The normalized propagation delay parameter $a$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    a = \frac{T_{\text{prop}}}{T_{\text{tx}}} = \frac{\tau \cdot B}{L_{\text{frame}}}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the channel bandwidth and $L_{\text{frame}}$ is the frame length in bits. As $a \to 0$, carrier sensing becomes instantaneous and channel efficiency approaches theoretical capacity. When $a \gg 1$, carrier sensing fails because stations sense outdated channel states.

\subsection{Non-Persistent CSMA}
In Non-Persistent CSMA, a station with a frame ready for transmission acts as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Senses the shared channel.
    \item If the channel is IDLE, the station transmits immediately.
    \item If the channel is BUSY, the station does not continuously monitor the channel. Instead, it waits for an independent random backoff interval drawn from a random distribution and then re-senses the channel.
\end{enumerate}
Because contending stations that sense a busy channel back off for different random durations, Non-Persistent CSMA substantially dampens the probability that multiple stations transmit simultaneously the moment a current transmission terminates. However, this randomness introduces idle slots when the channel is unoccupied, causing lower channel utilization under light loads. The theoretical throughput $S$ as a function of offered channel traffic $G$ (in frames per transmission time) is given by:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{Non-Pers}} = \frac{G \cdot e^{-a G}}{G(1 + 2a) + e^{-a G}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{1-Persistent CSMA}
In 1-Persistent CSMA, stations operate with maximum greediness:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Senses the shared channel.
    \item If the channel is IDLE, transmits immediately with probability $p = 1$.
    \item If the channel is BUSY, persists sensing the channel continuously until it detects the transition to IDLE, and then transmits immediately.
\end{enumerate}
Under light traffic, 1-Persistent CSMA incurs zero unnecessary idle waiting time. However, if two or more stations generate packets while another transmission is active, all waiting stations sense the channel becoming idle at the exact same instant and transmit simultaneously. This produces a \textbf{deterministic post-transmission collision cascade}. Theoretical throughput for 1-Persistent CSMA is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{1-Pers}} = \frac{G [1 + G + a G (1 + G + \frac{a G}{2})] e^{-G(1+2a)}}{G(1 + 2a) - (1 - e^{-a G}) + (1 + a G) e^{-G(1+a)}}
\end{equation}
Under the assumption of negligible propagation delay ($a \to 0$), this simplifies to:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\text{1-Pers}} = \frac{G (1 + G) e^{-G}}{G + e^{-G}}
\end{equation}
Under heavy traffic ($G \gg 1$), $S_{\text{1-Pers}} \to 0$ due to catastrophic collision cascades.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$p$-Persistent}{p-Persistent} CSMA and Slotted Arbitration}
The $p$-Persistent CSMA scheme combines the prompt responsiveness of 1-Persistent CSMA with the collision avoidance of Non-Persistent CSMA by operating on slotted contention boundaries (where slot length $\delta \ge \tau$):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item When a station has a packet and senses the channel idle, it transmits with probability $p$, or defers to the next slot with probability $q = 1 - p$.
    \item If it defers to the next slot, it senses the channel again:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If the slot is still IDLE, it transmits with probability $p$, or defers with probability $q$.
        \item If the channel has become BUSY (another station began transmitting), it acts as though a collision occurred and schedules retransmission according to backoff.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
If $N$ stations are ready to transmit when the channel clears, the probability that exactly one station successfully acquires the medium in the first slot is:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{success}} = N \cdot p \cdot (1 - p)^{N-1}
\end{equation}
The optimal probability $p^*$ that maximizes $P_{\text{success}}$ is derived by differentiating with respect to $p$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d P_{\text{success}}}{d p} = N(1-p)^{N-1} - N(N-1)p(1-p)^{N-2} = 0 \implies p^* = \frac{1}{N}
\end{equation}
Thus, under $N = 10$ contending stations, the optimal persistence probability lies in the region $p^* \approx 0.10$. Selecting $p \ll p^*$ wastes channel capacity on idle slots, while $p \gg p^*$ causes severe collision bursts.

\subsection{CSMA with Collision Detection (CSMA/CD)}
Pure CSMA protocols suffer from a major inefficiency: once two stations collide, they continue transmitting their complete frames to the end, wasting an entire transmission duration $T_{\text{tx}}$. In IEEE 802.3 Ethernet, stations implement \textbf{Collision Detection (CSMA/CD)}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Listen While Talking:} While transmitting, the transceiver continuously compares the transmitted signal with the channel state. If simultaneous transmission is detected, a collision is flagged.
    \item \textbf{Immediate Abort and Jam Signal:} Upon detecting a collision, the station halts transmission immediately and transmits a 32-to-48 bit \textbf{Jamming Signal} to ensure all contending stations detect the collision.
    \item \textbf{Truncated Binary Exponential Backoff (BEB):} Colliding stations defer their next attempt by $R$ slot times, where $R$ is drawn uniformly at random:
    \begin{equation}
        R \in [0, 2^k - 1], \quad \text{where } k = \min(\text{attempt}, 10)
    \end{equation}
    If collisions persist after 16 attempts ($\text{MAX\_COLLISIONS} = 16$), the frame is dropped with an unrecoverable medium error.
\end{enumerate}

To ensure that a station does not complete transmitting before the collision signal returns from the furthest point of the collision domain, the frame transmission time must exceed the round-trip propagation time:
\begin{equation}
    T_{\text{tx}} \ge 2 \cdot \tau \implies L_{\text{min}} \ge 2 \cdot \tau \cdot B = 2 \cdot d_{\text{max}} \cdot \frac{B}{v}
\end{equation}
This fundamental relationship dictates the 64-byte minimum frame size in standard IEEE 802.3 Ethernet.

\section{System Architecture and Procedural Design}

\subsection{3-Tier Client-Server Architecture Overview}
The medium access emulation environment is structured as a modular 3-tier client-server socket pipeline operating over TCP/IP loopback (\texttt{127.0.0.1:5000}). The architecture comprises three distinct entities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shared Channel Server (\texttt{server.c}):} Acts as the physical transmission medium. It manages the global channel state machine (\texttt{CH\_IDLE}, \texttt{CH\_BUSY}, \texttt{CH\_COLLISION}), multiplexes incoming carrier sense requests, detects simultaneous transmission overlaps, broadcasts jamming signals (in CSMA/CD) or drops collided frames naturally (in pure CSMA), and forwards clean frames to the destination receiver.
    \item \textbf{Contending Network Stations (\texttt{sender.c}):} Independent processes representing network interface cards (NICs). Each station reads from \texttt{input.txt}, segments data into 46-byte payloads, builds 65-byte Ethernet frames, executes carrier sensing, implements backoff, and tracks per-frame delay and collisions.
    \item \textbf{Destination Receiver Node (\texttt{receiver.c}):} Registers with the channel server, receives clean forwarded frames, unpacks headers, displays frame statistics, and issues 2-byte ACKs (\texttt{0xA5, seqNo}) for CSMA/CD transmissions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Channel State Machine (FSM)}
The shared channel server maintains an explicit finite state machine with three states:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{IDLE (\texttt{0}):} No station is transmitting. Carrier sensing replies with \texttt{CH\_IDLE}.
    \item \textbf{BUSY (\texttt{1}):} Exactly one station is transmitting. The channel remains occupied for $T_{\text{tx}} = 80\text{ ms}$. If another station transmits during this interval, a collision is flagged.
    \item \textbf{COLLISION (\texttt{2}):} Two or more transmissions overlap. For pure CSMA, the medium remains occupied for the remaining transmission duration and collided frames are naturally dropped. For CSMA/CD, the server broadcasts a 2-byte Jam Signal (\texttt{0xFE, 0x02}) to colliding stations and clears the medium after $T_{\text{jam}} = 20\text{ ms}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Protocol Opcodes and Channel Handshake}
The entities coordinate through standardized control opcodes defined in \texttt{csma\_common.h}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{MSG\_REGISTER\_RX} (\texttt{0x01}): Receiver registers with the server as the destination sink.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_REGISTER} (\texttt{0x05}): Sender registers its station name string.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_PROTO\_SYNC} (\texttt{0x06}) / \texttt{MSG\_SET\_PROTO} (\texttt{0x07}): The first connecting station selects the CSMA protocol; the server locks this selection and enforces it across all stations.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_SENSE\_REQ} (\texttt{0x10}) / \texttt{MSG\_SENSE\_RESP} (\texttt{0x11}): Sender senses carrier; server replies with \texttt{CH\_IDLE} or \texttt{CH\_BUSY}.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_TX\_START} (\texttt{0x20}): Dispatches a 65-byte packed Ethernet frame onto the medium.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_FWD\_FRAME\_NOACK} (\texttt{0x30}) / \texttt{MSG\_FWD\_FRAME\_ACK} (\texttt{0x31}): Server forwards frame to receiver.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_TX\_ACK} (\texttt{0xA5}): 2-byte ACK (\texttt{0xA5, seqNo}) relayed to the transmitting station.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_COLLISION} (\texttt{0xFE}): Jam signal broadcast to colliding stations in CSMA/CD.
    \item \texttt{MSG\_STATION\_DONE} (\texttt{0xFF}): Sender signals completion of all frames.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Carrier sensing and CSMA transmission arbitration (\texttt{sender.c}).}, label={lst:csma_sender}]
static int transmitFrameCSMA(sock_t sock, const char *stationName,
                             const EthernetFrame *frame, const unsigned char packedBuf[FRAME_SIZE],
                             CSMAProtocol proto, double p_prob, int *collision_count) {
    int attempts = 0;
    while (attempts < MAX_COLLISIONS) {
        /* Step A: Sense Carrier according to selected CSMA scheme */
        if (proto == PROTO_NON_PERSISTENT) {
            while (senseCarrier(sock) != CH_IDLE) {
                performBackoff(1, PROTO_NON_PERSISTENT);
            }
        } else if (proto == PROTO_1_PERSISTENT || proto == PROTO_CSMA_CD) {
            while (senseCarrier(sock) != CH_IDLE) {
                SLEEP_MS(2);
            }
        } else if (proto == PROTO_P_PERSISTENT) {
            while (1) {
                if (senseCarrier(sock) == CH_IDLE) {
                    double r = (double)rand() / (double)RAND_MAX;
                    if (r <= p_prob) break; /* Transmit with probability p */
                    else SLEEP_MS(SLOT_TIME_MS); /* Defer 1 slot with prob (1 - p) */
                } else {
                    SLEEP_MS(2);
                }
            }
        }

        /* Step B: Transmit 1-byte opcode + 65-byte packed EthernetFrame */
        unsigned char pkt[1 + FRAME_SIZE];
        pkt[0] = MSG_TX_START;
        memcpy(&pkt[1], packedBuf, FRAME_SIZE);
        if (send(sock, (const char *)pkt, sizeof(pkt), 0) <= 0) return 0;

        /* Step C: Protocol-Specific Post-Transmission Handling */
        if (proto == PROTO_CSMA_CD) {
            uint8_t reply[2] = {0, 0};
            if (recv_exact(sock, reply, 2) < 0) return 0;
            if (reply[0] == ACK_MARKER && reply[1] == frame->sequenceNumber) {
                return 1; /* Clean delivery and ACK received */
            }
            /* Jam signal (0xFE) received: immediate abort and BEB backoff */
            attempts++;
            (*collision_count)++;
            performBackoff(attempts, proto);
        } else {
            /* Pure CSMA: Wait full frame duration without ACK */
            SLEEP_MS(TX_TIME_MS);
            return 1;
        }
    }
    return 0;
}
\end{lstlisting}

    \item \textbf{Test Case 1: $p$-Persistent CSMA vs. Persistence Probability $p$ (Fixed $N = 10$):}

        \item \textit{Parameters:} $p \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.70, 0.80, 0.90, 1.00\}$; $N = 10$ stations; 2,000 delivered frames per run; 5 independent seed trials per parameter.
        \item \textit{Metrics:} Total collisions, average frame delay (ms), and channel throughput (\%).

    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Objective:} Compare the scaling resilience and channel stability of Non-Persistent, 1-Persistent, $p$-Persistent (at optimal $p = 0.20$), and CSMA/CD as network density increases.
        \item \textit{Parameters:} Contending stations $N \in \{2, 4, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30\}$; 2,000 delivered frames per protocol configuration averaged across 5 random seeds.
        \item \textit{Metrics:} Total collisions, average frame delay (ms), and channel throughput (\%).

    \item \textbf{Test Case 3: Live Socket Loopback Execution and Protocol Trace Verification:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Objective:} Execute compiled \texttt{server}, \texttt{receiver}, and independent \texttt{sender} station binaries natively over TCP loopback (\texttt{127.0.0.1:5000}) with the 49-frame \texttt{input.txt} dataset.
        \item \textit{Verification:} Capture live traces of carrier sensing, collision detection, jam signal broadcast (\texttt{0xFE}), Truncated Binary Exponential Backoff, and clean frame forwarding.

\section{Analysis and Discussion}

\subsection{Impact of Persistence Probability \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p} in \texorpdfstring{$p$-Persistent}{p-Persistent} CSMA (Test Case 1)}
Figure \ref{fig:case1_plots} and Table \ref{tab:case1} highlight the fundamental trade-off governed by $p$ under $N = 10$ contending stations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Low Persistence Regime ($p = 0.05$):} Collisions are minimized (234 collisions), but stations defer with probability $1-p = 0.95$ even when the medium is clear. Wasted idle slots elevate average transmission delay to 75.38 ms and constrain throughput to 65.90\%.
    \item \textbf{Optimal Operating Regime ($p^* \approx 0.10$):} In exact agreement with theoretical analysis ($p^* = 1/N = 1/10 = 0.10$), channel throughput attains its global maximum of \textbf{68.44\%} and average frame delay attains its minimum of \textbf{72.55 ms}.
    \item \textbf{High Persistence Regime ($p \ge 0.30 \to 1.00$):} As $p$ increases, multiple stations waiting for a busy frame attempt transmission immediately upon channel clearance. Collisions grow steadily from 234 ($p=0.05$) to \textbf{1,841} ($p=1.00$), average delay climbs to \textbf{96.75 ms}, and throughput degrades to \textbf{50.57\%}, converging directly to the behavior of 1-Persistent CSMA.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparative Protocol Efficiency across Contending Stations \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N} (Test Case 2)}
Figure \ref{fig:case2_plots} and Table \ref{tab:case2} demonstrate the operational characteristics of the four protocols as network density scales from $N = 2$ to $N = 30$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CSMA/CD Dominance:} CSMA/CD consistently delivers the highest throughput (\textbf{88.08\%} at $N=2$ and \textbf{68.89\%} at $N=30$) and lowest average transmission delay (\textbf{11.35 ms} to \textbf{209.47 ms}) across all station counts. While CSMA/CD experiences the same initial collision events as 1-Persistent CSMA (4,065 at $N=30$), aborting transmissions within $T_{\text{jam}} = 2\text{ slots}$ rather than transmitting full frames ($T_{\text{tx}} = 10\text{ slots}$) saves \textbf{80\% of wasted bandwidth per collision}.
    \item \textbf{1-Persistent Degradation:} Under light load ($N = 2$), 1-Persistent achieves 74.93\% throughput and low delay (13.34 ms). However, as $N$ increases, synchronized post-busy collisions escalate rapidly. At $N = 30$, average delay climbs to \textbf{443.89 ms} and throughput drops to \textbf{32.49\%}.
    \item \textbf{$p$-Persistent Scalability:} Setting $p = 0.20$ allows $p$-Persistent CSMA to outperform 1-Persistent and Non-Persistent across moderate station counts ($N = 4$ to $12$), achieving \textbf{70.92\%} throughput at $N = 4$ and \textbf{61.10\%} at $N = 10$.
    \item \textbf{Non-Persistent Channel Spacing:} By forcing stations that detect a busy channel to back off immediately, Non-Persistent CSMA avoids clustered transmissions. However, excessive random waiting intervals cause higher average delay under continuous traffic (535.23 ms at $N=30$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Known Limitations and Practical Enhancements}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hidden and Exposed Terminal Problems:} Carrier sensing assumes all stations can hear each other. In wireless environments, physical obstacles create hidden terminals. Modern IEEE 802.11 standards address this via CSMA/CA with Request-to-Send / Clear-to-Send (RTS/CTS) control frame handshaking.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Persistence Adaptation:} In our implementation, $p$ was fixed. Practical protocols dynamically estimate active stations $\hat{N}$ by observing channel idle/collision statistics and tune $p = 1/\hat{N}$ dynamically to maximize throughput.
\end{itemize}
